⚡ Flash ALOHA

Learning Fast and Precise Manipulation

John Hua Yao Qi Wu Yihuai Gao Chelsea Finn Zipeng Fu

Paper 🔧 Hardware Code arXiv (coming soon)

First presented as ALOHA Lightning at ICRA in Vienna on July, 2026

Abstract

Learning from human demonstrations has enabled robots to acquire a wide range of manipulation skills, but learned policies typically execute far slower than ordinary humans. This speed gap is mainly due to lack of an interface for precisely collecting demonstration data at high speed, and the difficulty in training policies that can robustly execute high-speed motions. In this paper, we present Flash ALOHA, a practical system for learning fast and precise robotic manipulation. Our system uses kinesthetic teaching to intuitively collect accurate and near-human-speed demonstrations on a backdrivable bimanual platform, yielding natural and fast trajectories. Our learning pipeline consists of two components: a lightweight smoothing algorithm to address tracking instabilities produced by action chunking, and for visual alignment we mask out the operator visible in the camera views. Given 50 demonstrations for each task, Flash ALOHA autonomously completes bimanual tasks such as ball tossing and catching, folding shorts, battery insertion, and bussing tables at over 80% success rates while close to human speed.
